Overlap = Dice: 1 = identical, 0 = none. Expert outline: the autoPET lesion outline, drawn on the hospital's own images (2 mm pixels), shown here on the 4 mm grid.
Same image, different volumes.
  • A fixed SUV of 2.5 ignores the tumour and the background: it grows into anything warm next to the tumour, and in a liver (background about 2.5) or the brain it fails completely. TG211 lists it among the poorest performers.
  • The EANM guideline: VOI41 "corresponds best with the actual dimensions of the tumour, but only for higher tumour-to-background values and nonheterogeneous tracer uptakes".
  • Adaptive methods "need to be calibrated for each PET scanner and acquisition/processing protocol" (Zaidi et al., in TG211). The 0.3 here is only an example.
In practice.
  • In lung, guidelines contour the GTV on CT, with PET as a guide to what is tumour (for example tumour against collapsed lung).
  • Automatic PET outlines are a starting point that a physician checks and edits; practice varies between sites and disease sites.
  • TG211: always check a method on your own scanner and protocol before clinical use.
The method determines the GTV. Ask which method, and at which window, made the contour you are given.

The threshold follows the maximum.
  • More iterations and no filter: a sharper, noisier image, so a higher SUVmax (one hot voxel).
  • 41 % of a higher maximum is a higher threshold, so the outline shrinks, with no change in the patient.
  • A fixed SUV 2.5 moves the other way: a sharper edge and more noise change what lies above 2.5.
