D looks like an ADC map: smooth, clear anatomy. f is speckled. D* is mostly noise. And in CSF the fit says f ≈ 0.9, which makes no sense: the model breaks down where its assumptions do not hold. This is why IVIM maps are hard to use, and why studies of f (for example as a hypoxia marker) disagree.

Simulated voxel: D = 1.0, f = 0.15, D* = 20 × 10⁻³ mm²/s. Each scan adds new noise; the fit then estimates D, f and D* (segmented fit: D from b ≥ 200, f from where that line meets b = 0, then D* from the low b-values).
Noise (signal-to-noise at b = 0)
The same voxel, scanned again and again: D stays close to 1.0, f wanders, and D* is all over the place. The maps you saw show the same thing across a whole brain.
Brain data: E. Peterson, IVIM dataset (21 b-values, 3 directions averaged), Figshare 2016, CC0, doi:10.6084/m9.figshare.3395704. Slice 25 of 54; D, f and D* maps are the author's own fit. Model: S(b)/S(0) = f·e−b·D* + (1 − f)·e−b·D (IVIM, Le Bihan et al., Radiology 1988). True-change limits: QIBA DWI Profile, 2022. Lowest b-value above 0: Kooreman et al., Radiother Oncol 2020.
