Liver noise (SD, as % of the mean)

Blue: the filter you chose (dot: the iterations on screen). Grey: the other filters. Hover a dot for its value.
There is no one right SUVmax.
  • The same counts give a different SUVmax with every iteration and filter. More iterations raise it partly because the hottest voxel gets noisier: SUVmax is one voxel.
  • The hospital used a sharper reconstruction (resolution modelling, time of flight, 2 mm pixels, 2 mm filter) than this standard one (4 mm pixels).
  • The liver mean hardly moves: averages over a big region are robust. Maxima of small things are not.
  • That is why multi-centre trials and EARL accreditation fix the reconstruction, so that a phantom gives the same numbers everywhere (next step). The same idea as fixing the b-values for ADC.
Same patient, same data: SUVmax depends on the reconstruction. Compare SUVs only between scans reconstructed the same way.

The partial-volume effect.
  • The blur spreads each sphere's counts over a bigger area: small spheres lose their peak.
  • Lesions smaller than 2–3 times the resolution (FWHM) read low (Soret et al.). Here that is below about 15–20 mm.
  • A tumour that shrinks under treatment reads a lower SUVmax even if each cell takes up as much FDG as before.
Small lesions read low. SUVmax depends on the lesion's size as well as its uptake: check the size before you compare.
